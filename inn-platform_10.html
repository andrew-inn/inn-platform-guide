What it does

One screen for every story's journey

Once you publish a story, it can reach many different audiences through many different channels — text subscribers, editors you trust, syndication partners — but most newsrooms manage each of these in a separate tool, or not at all. The INN Platform brings them together.

Every story you publish appears in Content Hub. From there, with a toggle or a quick approval step, it can route to any combination of channels. Performance from every channel comes back to the same row — no switching between dashboards.

Before you start

Three things to know

1. There are three kinds of people your stories can reach

Text subscribers
Readers who've signed up to receive your journalism by text message. They opted in on their own — you didn't recruit them individually.
→ Dashboard · Inbox · Broadcast · Settings
Network contacts
Editors and outlets you've personally invited to receive your approved stories by email. These are professional relationships you build and manage yourself.
→ My Network
Syndication partners
Platforms and networks — like Apple News, Plucky, or Ekkow — that have agreements with INN to distribute member content. You opt in once; approved stories route automatically.
→ Content Hub → Partner Settings

2. Not every story needs to go everywhere

You choose, for each story, which channels it goes to. Some take one click. Others need a short approval step first. Nothing routes automatically without your say-so.

3. Content Hub is the center of everything

Every distribution decision lives there. The other sections — Dashboard, Inbox, Broadcast, My Network, Settings, Pulse — either feed into Content Hub or show you results from it.

The core workflow

How a story moves through the platform

Five steps, all from one screen
1
Story is published by your newsroom and appears automatically in Content Hub.
2
Choose your channels — every distribution option is available from the story's row. Toggle to queue it for your text subscriber digest. Toggle again to include it in your next scheduled email to editors. Both take one click and don't require anything else.
3
Approve for syndication partners — if you want the story to route to INN-managed platforms, click Approve. You'll add an image, a caption, and a photo credit. The Approve button activates once all three are complete.
4
Stories route automatically from that point forward — to the digest, to your editor contacts on their schedule, to syndication partners for pickup.
5
Performance comes back to the same row — click counts from your text subscribers, open rates from your editor network, and pickup status from each syndication partner all appear in the story's row without switching tools.
On the approval step: Partners have display standards. An image with a caption and credit is required because stories without them are often rejected or displayed incorrectly on partner platforms. Queuing a story for your text digest or your editor network does not require this step — those channels are independent.
Section 1

Dashboard

Your text subscriber program at a glance. Everything here reflects readers who receive your news by text — how many have subscribed this month, how many clicks links in your texts have gotten, how many messages have gone out, and which stories are driving the most engagement.

The chart below the stat tiles shows six months of subscriber growth. The ranked list at the bottom shows your top-performing stories by click count from text subscribers. These links will eventually connect directly to each story in Content Hub.

Section 2

Inbox

Where text conversations live. When someone texts in to subscribe — or texts back with a question, a response, or their profile information — it shows up here.

The Inbox has three columns:

Messages
A list of all conversations, sorted by most recent. Shows each person's name (if they've provided it) or their phone number, a preview of the last message, and an unread indicator. Use the search field to find a specific subscriber by name, number, or zip code.
Conversation
The full message thread with the selected subscriber. Incoming messages appear on the left; your replies appear on the right. Type a reply in the field at the bottom and click Send. Your reply goes directly to their phone.
Subscriber profile
Name, email, zip code, and stated interests — filled in when a subscriber responds to the sign-up questions. You can edit any field and save. The panel also shows subscription status, join date, and how many digests they've received. You can unsubscribe someone from here if needed.

Where profile information comes from

When someone texts in to subscribe, they receive a welcome message asking for their first name, zip code, and topics they're interested in. If they reply, that information is automatically parsed into their profile fields. Not everyone responds — so some profiles will only have a phone number.

Setting up INN Texting for your newsroom: The Inbox only works if your newsroom has an active INN Texting line. If you haven't set one up yet, the full setup guide covers everything from creating a landing page to growing your subscriber list to managing your weekly digest.

Section 3

Content Hub

The main working screen. Every story, every distribution channel, in one view. This is where you'll spend most of your time.

Content Hub has two tabs at the top: Articles & Distribution (your daily workflow) and Partner Settings (one-time setup for syndication partners).

Articles & Distribution

A list of all your published stories. Six summary numbers run across the top — stories published, live syndication placements, total tracked reads from partners, clicks from your text digest, republications, and stories waiting for your action (in amber).

Use the filter chips to see only what needs attention. Search by headline. Every channel for a story is in its row:

ColumnWhat it showsWhat you do
INN TextingWhether the story is queued for your next text digest, and how many clicks it got once sentToggle to queue — one click, no approval needed
My NetworkWhether the story is queued for your next editor email, and open count once sentToggle to queue — one click, no approval needed
Syndication & PartnersStatus with each INN-managed partner: sent / in review / live with click count / killedApprove the story first, then partners become eligible
PickupsTotal tracked reads across all syndication partnersRead-only

Stories needing approval have an amber border. Approved stories show a green badge. Killed stories are faded with no action buttons.

The approval step

Click any story headline or the Approve button. A panel opens where you upload at least one image, add a caption, and add a photo credit. A checklist tracks what's still needed — the Approve button stays grayed out until all three are complete. Once you approve, the story routes to every syndication partner you've opted into.

Partner Settings

The second tab inside Content Hub. This is where you opt your newsroom into INN-managed syndication partners. Each partner has its own content-sharing agreement — read it before accepting. You'll receive an emailed copy of every agreement you sign. You can opt out at any time, and opting into one partner doesn't affect any other.

Section 4

Broadcast

Send a one-time text message to all of your text subscribers. Use this for breaking news, severe weather, or anything that can't wait for the next weekly digest.

A Broadcast is different from the INN Texting queue in Content Hub. The queue assembles your weekly digest automatically from stories you've toggled in. A Broadcast is a manual message you write and send yourself, and it goes out immediately.

Type your message, check the character counter (160 characters = one text message), and click Send Broadcast. The Recent Broadcasts list below shows what you've sent, when, and to how many text subscribers.

Section 5

My Network

Your personal list of editors and outlets who receive your approved stories by email. These are professional relationships you've built yourself — not text subscribers, and not INN's managed syndication partners. You build this list, you set the schedule, and you own these relationships entirely.

Adding contacts

Enter a name, organization, and email at the bottom of the contact list. They receive an invitation and begin getting your story emails once they confirm. Each contact shows their open rate and how many stories they've received.

Setting schedules

Every contact has their own send schedule — which days of the week and what time they receive your stories. Click the schedule pill in any contact's row to edit it. Toggle individual days on or off; weekends are off by default but can be added. The "Weekdays" button resets to Monday–Friday. The Default Schedule card at the top applies to new contacts automatically — override per person from there.

Recent sends

A history of story emails sent to your network, with open rates and click counts per send.

Section 6

Settings

Two things: invite team members to the platform, and configure the automated messages your text subscribers receive.

Invite a team member — enter their name and email and they'll receive access. You can have multiple editors managing the platform.

Newsroom messages — three templates that go to text subscribers automatically:

Weekly intro
Sent at the top of each Friday digest. Update it each week if you want to personalize it.
Welcome message
Sent automatically when someone new texts in to subscribe. This is the first thing they see — make it welcoming and clear.
Thank you message
Sent after a new subscriber provides their name, zip, and interests. Confirms you've received their info.

These are different from Broadcasts — they run automatically without you doing anything once they're configured.

Full setup guidance: The INN Texting setup guide has exact template language and recommendations for each of these messages. Read the guide →
Section 7

Pulse

Your publishing health at a glance. Pulse answers one question: are you publishing at your normal pace, or has something shifted?

Four numbers at the top show your story count for the last 30 days, how many days you published, your deviation from your 26-week rolling average (in red if you're behind), and your six-month total with a note on how many stories have been syndicated.

A signal label below — on track, surging, or contracting — gives you a quick read on your momentum. A 28-day bar chart shows publishing day by day. Blue bars are days you published; gray bars are days you didn't. A dotted line shows your baseline pace.

Recent stories appear to the right of the chart with topic labels, dates, and a "syndicated" marker for any story a partner has picked up. Pulse is read-only — it updates automatically based on your publishing and distribution activity.

If you're setting up for the first time
1
Set up INN Texting if you haven't already. The full setup guide covers onboarding, creating a landing page, building your subscriber list, and managing your weekly digest. Read the INN Texting setup guide →
2
Go to Content Hub → Partner Settings and review the available syndication partners. Read each agreement before accepting. Opt into the ones that fit your newsroom.
3
Go to My Network and add the editors and outlets you have existing relationships with. Set each person's send schedule.
4
Go to Settings and update the three automated messages your text subscribers receive — especially the welcome message, since that's the first thing new subscribers see.
5
Go to Content Hub and work through any stories flagged as needing approval. Add images, captions, and credits and approve them. After that, your daily workflow is checking Content Hub — approving stories, toggling channels, and watching clicks and pickups come in.
